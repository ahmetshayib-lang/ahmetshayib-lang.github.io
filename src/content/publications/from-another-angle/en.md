## About the book

How often have you judged someone from a single scene? How many ideas do you defend not because you examined them, but because you heard them so often they became part of you?

The book offers no ready-made answers. Instead it hands the reader questions: about the way we see the world, the first impression we mistake for a final verdict, the convictions that reached us fully formed, the silence we choose to stay inside the group, the comparison that steals our own path, and the definition of success that someone else wrote.

## How each chapter works

Each chapter opens with **a scene from life** that may resemble something you have lived. Then comes **an idea worth pausing on**, **another angle** that runs against your first reaction, and **questions** you are not asked to answer quickly. Each chapter closes with **“a sip of meaning”** — a few lines at the end of the conversation.

The book draws on studies in psychology and sociology, and on verses of the Qur'an and hadith, all referenced to their sources. It is written in Arabic.

## The six parts

1. What do we actually see?
2. Ideas that aren't ours
3. The person we don't know
4. The decisions that make us
5. The things we make bigger than they are
6. Widening the view

> “Don't read this book quickly.”
